Abstract (Intisari)
Poorly managed mental workload in the digital transformation era contributes significantly
to the employee turnover phenomenon, which compromises organizational stability and
balance. This study aims to conduct a comparative performance analysis of predictive
models for employee turnover intention using two ensemble learning algorithms: Random
Forest (RF) and Hist Gradient Boosting (Hist GB). An empirical dataset of employee mental
workload was processed through a series of machine learning stages, including feature
engineering to construct a work-hours-to-sleep ratio and composite mental load (total mental
load). The severe class imbalance challenge, where the target class proportion stands at a
68% (stay) to 32% (leave intention) ratio, was addressed using the Synthetic Minority Over-
sampling Technique (SMOTE). Models were optimized via hyperparameter tuning and
validated using 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation. Experimental results based on real
program data demonstrate that the Base scenario yields a pseudo accuracy (~69%) with a
very low F1-Score (~27%). The SMOTE intervention successfully rescued the models from
majority class bias, with the SMOTE-optimized Random Forest outperforming in
generalization stability, achieving a Mean CV Accuracy of 74.47%. On the other hand, Hist
Gradient Boosting (Optimized) produced the best minority class detection performance,
reaching the highest Test F1-Score of 52.60% and maintaining an AUC Score of 0.68.
Feature Rank analysis identified burnout score, deadline pressure score, and stress score as
the three most crucial indicators in triggering turnover intention.

1. Introduction
Based on datasets regarding the conditions of physical,
mental, and emotional exhaustion resulting from chronic
occupational stress experienced by professionals in the
technology industry. This condition arises due to heavy
pressure, long working hours, rapid expectations, and urgent
deadlines in the sector brought about by rapid digital
technological advances in modern industrial environments.
Such conditions have transformed how organizations view
operations while simultaneously triggering an escalation in
mental workload for employees. The pressure to adapt to
emerging technologies, dynamic target fluctuations, and the
blurring boundaries between workplace and personal life in
remote work arrangements have exacerbated workers'
psychological vulnerability (Ambrose & Qayum, 2024).
Cumulative mental workload that is not appropriately
mitigated gradually turns into chronic burnout (Jeong et al.,
2025), decreased productivity levels (Indriya, Fazlurrahman, &
Fitro, 2024), and psychological distress disorders (Bakkeli,
2023).
The most detrimental consequence of this mental well-being
destruction for corporate sustainability is the surge in
employee turnover intensity (Celbis, Wong, Kourtit, &
Nijkamp, 2023), which has been reflected globally on a
massive scale through "The Great Resignation" phenomenon
(Celbiş et al., 2023). When a competent employee resigns, the
organization not only loses intellectual capital (human capital)
but also incurs substantial financial costs for re-recruitment
and onboarding training (Laksawut & Narkbunnum, 2025).
Therefore, the ability to precisely map psychological
determinants and predict turnover propensity has become a
vital strategic agenda for modern human resource
management.
Amidst the complexity of these challenges, big data
repositories and Machine Learning techniques offer
revolutionary instruments to convert logistics data into
strategic decision-making foundations. Computational analytic
approaches facilitate the identification of hidden patterns
within workers' psychosocial variables that are difficult to
extract via conventional statistical methods (Luo et al., 2024).
Previous research has demonstrated the effectiveness of
machine learning algorithms in evaluating employee
performance (Bahman & Lysenko, 2025), predicting retention
(Mishra et al., 2025), and measuring job satisfaction across
multiple sectors (Fang, Chen, & Shi, 2025). Through
predictive modeling, HR managers can shift from reactive
handling to proactive, preventative intervention strategies
before turnover decisions are executed.
Numerous prior studies have affirmed the robustness of
supervised machine learning algorithms in analyzing
workforce behavior and psychological metrics. For instance,
research by Guo et al. (2026) explored turnover intention
intensity among Australian school principals using Extreme
Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). Meanwhile, research led by
Jeong et al. (2025) leveraged machine learning paired with
Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) to detect burnout
syndrome based on the Korean Occupational Stress Scale
(KOSS). Severe burnout and acute stress have also been
precisely detected through behavioral sensors in call center
agents (Lee & Swain, 2026) and computer vision monitoring
of control room operators (Ji et al., 2025).
Furthermore, Laksawut and Narkbunnum (2025) proposed
calibrated turnover risk models for capacity-constrained
retention triage, while Indriya et al. (2024) evaluated the
efficacy of mental health policy interventions on worker
productivity using Gradient Boosting and Random Forest
architectures. Collectively, this body of literature confirms that
tree-based ensemble models are highly effective and suitable
for processing non-linear, multi-level survey data of
psychosocial and organizational behavior.

In the context of this study, the Random Forest (RF) and
Hist Gradient Boosting (Hist GB) algorithms were chosen as
the primary comparative subjects because both ensemble
learning models possess complementary comparative
advantages in tabular data processing. Random Forest operates
by constructing an ensemble of independent decision trees in
parallel through bagging and majority voting aggregation,
rendering it exceptionally robust against overfitting risks
(Bakkeli, 2023).
Conversely, Hist Gradient Boosting applies sequential
boosting principles that optimize the loss function of
predecessor trees, with an innovation of grouping numerical
features into discrete bins (histograms) to significantly
accelerate the training pipeline on large-scale datasets. This
comparative study also integrates advanced processing
techniques such as composite feature engineering, class
imbalance mitigation using SMOTE, and hyperparameter
optimization to extract the upper performance boundaries of
both algorithms.
Against this background, the researchers intend to provide
novel contributions in understanding turnover intention based
on employee mental workload indicators through
comprehensive empirical evaluations. By employing a three-
scenario modeling comparison (Base Model, SMOTE Model,
and Optimized Model), this research deeply investigates the
extent of classification performance enhancement achievable
through hyperparameter tuning and data balancing
manipulation.
The main contribution of this research lies in the visualization
and empirical proof of the best model accuracy confirmed
through AUC-ROC curves and Confusion Matrix
visualizations, as well as establishing the hierarchy of the most
influential mental workload variables via Feature Rank
diagrams. The final outcome of this research is expected to
formulate a valid and dependable predictive model as an
organizational strategic intervention instrument in curbing
employee turnover rates within an enterprise across specific
operational periods.

The dataset utilized in this research was obtained from 2026
empirical data measuring mental workload levels,
psychological health conditions, and turnover intention
intensity among technology sector personnel (tech workers).

employee data spanning diverse job roles such as Software
Engineer, Data Scientist, and Product Manager (Thalla, 2026).

library versions of Python and Scikit-Learn utilized in this

2.2. Preprocessing & Feature Engineering
Before feeding data into the model training architecture,
data preprocessing and feature engineering steps were carried
out to bolster data informativeness. The employee_id
attribute was removed from the dataset as it represents a
unique identifier with no correlation to the classification target.
Categorical attributes such as gender, country,
job_role, seniority_level, company_size,
industry, and work_mode were converted into numeric
representations using LabelEncoder to allow ingestion by
both ensemble algorithms.
A crucial stage in this preprocessing was the formulation of
two novel composite features:
1. work_hours_to_sleep_ratio: Obtained from
dividing work_hours_per_week by the cumulative total
sleep hours across the week (sleep_hours_per_night
× 7). This feature extracts the imbalance interaction between
occupational time burden and biological recovery.
2. total_mental_load: Represents the linear
summation of four key mental workload indicators: stress
score (stress_score), job burnout (burnout_score),
depression score (phq9_score), and anxiety score
(gad7_score). This composite feature amplifies the
information signal of cumulative employee psychological
distress.

The modeling experiments in this study were systematically
designed across three testing scenarios for each respective
algorithm (Random Forest and Hist Gradient Boosting), with
the following workflows:
Base Model Scenario: Trains the Random Forest and Hist
Gradient Boosting algorithms using scikit-learn default library
parameters without class imbalance handling.
SMOTE Model Scenario: Applies Synthetic Minority
Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) to the training split to
equalize target class (job_change_intention)
distributions by generating synthetic samples in the minority
class, followed by model training under default parameters.
Optimized Model Scenario: Integrates SMOTE and
conducts targeted parameter search (hyperparameter tuning).
For Random Forest, tuned parameters include
n_estimators=200, max_depth=15, and
min_samples_split=5. For Hist Gradient Boosting,
parameters were optimized at max_iter=150,
max_depth=10, and learning_rate=0.05.
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Target Data Descriptive Analysis
Prior to evaluating classification performance, an analysis
of the target class distribution was conducted to determine the
degree of dataset imbalance. The visualization of the target
data distribution from the dataset is depicted in Figure 2.
Based on empirical data extraction, it was found that
employees intending to stay (Class 0) significantly dominated
compared to employees intending to change jobs (Class 1),
with a ratio near 68% versus 32%. This proportional
discrepancy reinforces the urgency of adopting SMOTE in the
experimental pipeline to prevent biased models (which tend to
accurately predict the majority class while completely failing
to identify the minority class).
This massive disproportion holds substantial potential to
induce computational bias, where the model accurately guesses
the majority class yet exhibits total failure in detecting warning
signals from employees at risk of resigning. Therefore, data
balancing techniques like SMOTE across experimental
scenarios act as vital interventions ensuring the model remains
untrapped by majority bias.
To ensure computational reproducibility and allow study
replication by other researchers, all comparative experiments
were executed within a standardized computing environment.
Data science workflows, feature engineering, SMOTE
balancing, and hyperparameter tuning were run on the Spyder
IDE platform under Python version 3.10. Primary libraries
utilized for ensemble learning architectures and metric
evaluations include Scikit-Learn version 1.3.0, supported by
Imbalanced-learn version 0.11.0 for SMOTE implementation,

4. Conclusion
Based on the experimental outcomes and comparative analyses
conducted, it can be concluded that:
1. The employee mental workload dataset exhibits severe
class imbalance with a 68% (stay) to 32% (turnover intention)
proportion, causing the Base scenario to yield computational
bias characterized by pseudo accuracy (~69%) with a dismal
F1-Score (~27%).
2. Data balancing intervention utilizing SMOTE effectively
rescues the model from majority class bias. The SMOTE-
optimized Random Forest excelled in generalization stability
with the highest Mean CV Accuracy of 74.47%.

achieved the best minority class detection performance with

Feature Importance extraction confirms that psychosocial
